Impressive Finishes

The new site

A website that matches the quality of the work.

Impressive Finishes is a family-owned painting company in North Metro Atlanta, in business since 1990. We rebuilt their website from the ground up, and it’s now live at impressivefinishes.com.

Where it started

The previous site.

A WordPress site with a single Services page. The opening screen had no phone number, the headline sat in a see-through panel over a photo, and the FAQ lived at a page called /home-2/.

Services

One page for each service.

Interior painting, exterior painting, drywall and carpentry repair, and deck and fence staining each have their own page with photos, process and FAQs, so a homeowner can go straight to the job they need.

Projects

Their own work, front and center.

A gallery of the company’s own project photos that visitors can filter by exteriors, interiors, and decks and porches, with a larger view of each photo.

Estimates

An estimate request that’s easy to finish.

Five required fields, a “What happens next” list beside the form, and a confirmation only once the request has actually been delivered.

On phones

Call, text or ask for an estimate from any page.

The previous phone view led with a large text panel and one “Contact Us Today” button. Now Call, Text and Free Estimate buttons stay at the bottom of the screen on every page.

Launch

Launched without losing what worked.

We moved the site to new hosting, kept their Google business email in place, and redirected old WordPress addresses, including a broken homepage link, to the right pages.

Early result. The owner reported two leads within the first few days after launch.

Project details
  • Organized services. A page for each main service (interior painting, exterior painting, drywall and carpentry repair, and deck and fence staining), plus a services overview, project gallery, service areas, FAQ, about and contact pages.
  • Clearer first impression. The opening screen says what the company does and where, with the phone number and a “Get a Free Estimate” button in the header, in a deep green and cream palette suited to an established family business.
  • Easier on phones. Call, text and “free estimate” buttons stay in reach at the bottom of the screen on phones. A skip-to-content link helps keyboard users.
  • Simpler estimate requests. An estimate request form that asks for the details a painter needs to quote, plus an FAQ covering warranty, insurance, pricing and preparation.
  • A careful move. The site moved from WordPress to new hosting. Old addresses redirect to their new pages, and the company’s Google business email stayed in place.

ProGlide Academy

The new site

A first website for a drone pilot course.

ProGlide Academy is an FAA Part 107 course taught by a working commercial drone pilot. There was no site before, so we built one around his own aerial footage.

From the field

Proof from real jobs.

A gallery of his aerial photos, from night concrete pours and resorts to waterfront homes and top-down construction shots, alongside his showreel and a site-visit time-lapse.

On phones

The same view, in your pocket.

The headline and full-width buttons stack over the drone footage, and the site is built to load fast on phones.

Overview

A course you can scan.

All 20 course modules sit in five sections that open and close, with counters for the modules, practice exams and flashcard sets.

Project details
  • His own footage, front and center. The opening screen plays a clip from the instructor’s drone showreel behind a camera-viewfinder overlay, so visitors see the view from his drone before they read a word.
  • Proof from real jobs. A “From the field” section with his full showreel, a site-visit time-lapse and a gallery of his aerial photos: night concrete pours, resorts, waterfront homes and top-down construction shots.
  • A curriculum you can scan. All 20 course modules grouped into five sections that open and close, with counters for the modules, practice exams and flashcard sets, and a “boarding pass” showing what’s included.
  • Details that fit the subject. A scroll altimeter that climbs to 400 feet (the legal drone ceiling) as you read, and a flight path that draws itself through the steps from enrolling to getting licensed. It’s built to load fast on phones.
